The River Never Returned Our Letters

By Madhusudan Mishra

There was a time when love travelled on folded paper,
when a heartbeat waited for footsteps instead of a ringtone,
when the shy meeting of two young eyes
could become the beginning of an endless universe.
In the fading sunlight of a village school,
a boy and a girl fell silently, beautifully, forever in love.
It was the late nineteen-seventies.
The world moved gently, untouched by glowing screens.
There were no instant messages, no hurried calls,
only stolen glances across the classroom,
shared smiles beneath old banyan trees,
and dreams too innocent to be spoken aloud.
Every evening the boy sat beneath a flickering lantern,
writing letters scented with hope and youthful longing.
Each page carried a promise of tomorrow,
each word whispered her name like a sacred prayer.
He believed love written with honesty
could never lose its way.
Trusting a classmate with his heart,
he asked another girl to place the letters in her hands.
But jealousy has often worn the mask of friendship.
One by one, the letters were torn into fragile pieces
and scattered upon the waiting river,
where the current swallowed every unspoken dream.

The girl waited through changing seasons.
She wondered why his silence had become so deep.
The boy waited through sleepless nights,
believing she had quietly rejected his love.
Neither knew that fate had borrowed the face of betrayal,
leaving two faithful hearts to suffer apart.
The years drifted like autumn leaves upon forgotten water.
Their school became a memory,
their laughter an echo lost among distant hills.
The boy grew into a man who never married,
for no other heart could replace
the first one that had become his forever home.
More than three decades slipped into history.
One quiet afternoon, destiny returned wearing tears.
The woman who had once carried those letters
stood before the old man, her voice trembling beneath the weight
of guilt.
& I have come too late,& she whispered,
& but I can no longer hide the truth.&

& I never gave her your letters.
I tore every one beside the river.
She waited for you. She loved you.
Had she received even one page,
she would have chosen you without hesitation.
Forgive me, if forgiveness still exists.&
The old man looked beyond the horizon,
where memory and twilight held each other&#39;s hands.
Not a single tear escaped his eyes;
some griefs become too ancient for weeping.
After a long silence, he smiled with heartbreaking tenderness.

& If she truly wished to spend her life with me,& he said,
& then come... let us walk once more to the riverbank.
Perhaps the water still remembers
the pieces of those forgotten letters.
Perhaps love never truly drowns—
it only waits for hearts brave enough to seek it again.&
And the river flowed, as rivers always do,
carrying secrets that no calendar can erase.
The torn letters were never found,
yet somewhere between the sky and the water,
two young lovers still walk home from school together,
their hands almost touching, their dreams forever unwritten,
while eternity gently whispers—
true love may be delayed by time, betrayed by fate, and
buried by silence,
but it can never be destroyed.
