Where Your Memory Still Sits

By Madhusudan Mishra

On the first morning of school,
before the bells had learned our names,
I found you in the front row—
a bob of hair, a morning smile,
and a voice as soft as rain
falling upon an unfamiliar garden.

You spoke with your friends,
and though I was only passing through
the ordinary noise of childhood,
something in your laughter
made the morning pause.
Some moments arrive without announcement;
years later, we discover
they were beginnings.
You carried brilliance quietly.
Every lesson opened before you,
every difficult question
seemed to surrender its secret.
And English, especially,
rested upon your lips
like a language the heart
had taught you before the books did.
You were not merely a bright student;
you were the light by which
many of us measured our own horizons.
While we counted marks,
you seemed to collect possibilities—
and excellence followed you
without ever needing to be called.
Then school became a memory,
and college opened its wider doors.
The corridors changed,
the books grew heavier,
the dreams became taller,
but you remained the same quiet flame.
Again, you rose to the summit,
and the old legacy of brilliance
continued its unbroken journey.
Somewhere between those years,
admiration changed its name.
What began as a passing wonder
slowly became a tenderness
I carried without confession—
a small, unspoken hope
hidden beneath ordinary days.
I tried to walk a little closer,
to place my footsteps
beside the road you travelled.
But destiny is an invisible river;
it chooses its own direction.

You were already beyond my pitch,
and the ball of possibility
vanished somewhere
beyond the boundary of time.
No tragedy followed.
Only distance.
Life gathered its seasons,
and we followed different stars.
Faces came and went,
dreams changed their addresses,
and the years quietly built
their walls between yesterday and today.
Yet some memories
do not live by the laws of time.
They remain exactly where we left them—
young, luminous, untouched.
And so, when nostalgia
opens the old doors of my mind,
I return to that classroom:
the sunlight on the desks,
the murmur of young voices,
the front row,
your smiling face,
and that gentle voice
I never knew I would remember
for so long.
I no longer wonder
what might have been.
Some people are not written
into the future of our lives;
they are written into
the poetry of our becoming.
You are one such presence—
not a destination,
not an unfinished promise,
but a beautiful line
that time could never erase.
And perhaps that is enough.
For whenever memory turns back
towards the first page of youth,
there you are again—
smiling in the front row,
while the years pass silently by,
and somewhere within me,
that morning
is still beginning.
