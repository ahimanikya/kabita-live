The Girl Who Walks in My Ink

By Annwesa Abhipsa Pani

I write because silence gnaws the walls.
The words come like insects—
they know where the wounds are.

Somewhere a girl was found
in the half-light of a banyan tree,
her small body folded like torn prayer paper.
The newspapers said nothing new—
only the familiar syllables of shame and shock.
Still, I cannot sleep without hearing her breath
stuck in the dirt.

Her hand was made of dusk.
How will I hold it?
Each time I try,
my fingers touch only ash and apology.

The streets outside blink with blind lamps,
their light trembling over puddles
as though the city itself is afraid to see.
Even the dogs have stopped barking.

They said she was fifteen.
I do not know her name,
only the way her story entered mine—
like smoke into lungs that never asked to breathe.
She had no voice left,
so mine began to stutter.

I write her because I cannot touch her.
Because if I did,
the weight of my guilt would split my arms.
Because her skin, in my mind,
is stitched with all the nights we refused to watch.

And when the page is full,
I see her shadow bending
over every word I made.
She does not forgive me.
She does not need to.
She just stands there,
half darkness, half dawn,
and waits for me
to finish writing her name.
