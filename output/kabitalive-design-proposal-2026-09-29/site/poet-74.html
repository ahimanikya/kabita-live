Aneek Chatterjee

Life and writing.

Aneek Chatterjee writes poetry and fiction alongside his work in international relations. His publisher’s biography records a doctorate in that subject and a Fulbright visit to the University of Virginia. His poetry collections include Seaside Myopia, Unborn Poems and Yellow Prison, and Archive Avenue; The Funeral Procession is a novel.

His Kabita Live poems move between intimate memory and public unease. “Warmth” recalls a clay oven and an elder sister through the ache of their absence, while “Blindfolded” exposes the uneasy comfort of refusing to look at injustice. In “Waiting For The Red Soil On My Skin,” the speaker imagines an education and a society renewed through water, soil and trees; “Future” makes room for uncertainty rather than a predetermined life.
