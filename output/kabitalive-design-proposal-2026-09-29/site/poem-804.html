WINGS WITHOUT A HARBOUR

By Meenakshi Goswami

Off and on I savor the pure delight
of slipping far from those sharp-tongued companions
and dissolving into the deep green hush of pine-scented woods
or the quiet sweep of open, peaceful ground.
I simply roam the wild places,
never asking what or why,
yet somehow I rise on invisible wings
and climb the endless blue that has no edge.
I let myself float free—
sometimes carried by the loud crash of waves,
sometimes by the soft, repeating pulse of gentler water—
for I am only a wanderer
with no guiding blade,
and I never look for any safe place to rest.
I long to sing the old country tunes,
missing notes as they come,
laughing and squealing with pure delight
as the sound rises—
sometimes high and light, sometimes low and deep—
and none of it matters to me.
I step across the bright green undergrowth,
the rough gray heights of stone,
the soft, sorrowful fields of grass,
and I pass the flowers whose strange and foreign names
I never learn.
I simply move on.
All the while I wonder
who this self might be,
what purpose holds me here,
or why the great earth keeps turning.
Still I remain glad—
whirling beside the small fire of my camp,
joking along the dusty tracks
that begin nowhere

and lead to nowhere at all...
