The Arrival of Spring

By Marmasparshee Priyadarshini

She folded a letter to the sky
and called it spring,
a paper boat of wishes
sailing through blue.

In it, she wrote of
gulmohar lanes,
jasmine breaths,
and the lavender hues,
once lost in the wind.

She pressed petals between lines,
each word tinted with tulip blush,
each comma a falling leaf of apricot.

And then,
a hummingbird carried a full stop
in its beak
and dropped it on her windowsill.

The sky read slowly,
turning pages
with breeze-fingers,
raining down pauses and pollen.

That night,
the moon blinked twice
as if it knew the reply
was dew-dropping beneath cloud-veils.

By morning,
her garden had grown
a new language,
one only butterflies could read.

And so, every spring,
she writes to the sky,
knowing even letters wear flowers.
