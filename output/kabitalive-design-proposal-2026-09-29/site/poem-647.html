Once I belonged to a zamindar family

By Sumana Bhattacharjee

Once I belonged to a zamindar family
As my ancestors said we l are blue-blooded and it`s our  legacy.

But I feel the life I`m living now, it`s me
We`re now  fighting for bread and butter
And it`s harsh reality,
I do admit it without any hypocracy.

Gone those days
When we didn`t believe in mediocrity,
We belonged to that elit class
Who never thought about  simplicity,
Aristocracy was the utmost priority.

My great Grandpa used to say
Simplicity and mediocrity, these words are not made for us,
We belong to another class.

Abruptly, one day I said
" See Grandpa, looking so amazing those belwari glass,
Then smiled  and said "But full of rust and dust " won`t last.

He turn around  and said
"Whatever we are , it`s your  identity
"It`s your father`s advance thought and sagacity
That reflected in you and fueled  your audacity".

Further he said, "In  society, we have another place
Girl! Never try it to replace."

I murmured, " Yes, a dimly lit ancient place
Even light refuses to spread,
People are not allowed to see our face
Our feet binded in  silken thread.

I`m only trying to make some changes
Can`t do much, I`m not that great.

I`m the last daughter
Of this legacy,
Who refused to stay in fantasy.

The world is transforming fast,

And I  chose mediocrity over aristocracy.
