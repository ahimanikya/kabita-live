Sashisena Temple, Sonepur – A Song of Love, Liberation, and Legacy

By Madhusudan Mishra

— A Poem in English Inspired by the Sacred Soil of Sonepur:
In the heart of Sonepur’s silent land,
Stands a shrine, so calm, so grand.
No doors it holds, no walls to bind,
A temple open to all of humankind.
Built in the year of nineteen twenty,
Not with gold, but love in plenty.
It tells the tale of a royal flame,
Of Sashisena and Ahi Manikya’s name.
She, the princess — fierce and free,
He, a minister’s son — brave as can be.
Their hearts met like the monsoon rain,
Flowing through joy, flowing through pain.
But fate was cruel, and spells were cast,
By Jema Dei, of the tantric past.
Ahi turned sheep by light of day,
By night, a man — in shadows’ sway.
Yet love stood tall, it did not break,
Not spells, nor fear, could it forsake.
In forests deep, in nights of cries,
Their love still danced beneath the skies.
This temple rose not for gods above,
But for the power of human love.
No priestly throne, no grand pretense,
Just silent walls with deep incense.
And in her name, the winds still sing,
Of freedom&#39;s flight, of woman’s wing.
For Sashisena, with spirit wide,
Chose her path, her love, her pride.
She spoke not words in whispered tone,
But stood for dreams she called her own.
In a world that told her to obey,
She carved her truth, her rightful way.
This temple now, a voice of grace,
For every woman in every place.
To rise, to love, to break all chain,
To walk through fire and dance through rain.

So come, dear soul, and bow your head,
Not just for gods, or tales once said.
But for a girl who dared to be,
A symbol of love — and of being free.
Nimuhi Mandir — a doorless shrine, where love walks in, unmarked by time.
A call to the heart, to remember that love liberates, and truth shines.
A woman once loved here — now, she leads.
Sashisena still lives — in every woman who dares to dream.
