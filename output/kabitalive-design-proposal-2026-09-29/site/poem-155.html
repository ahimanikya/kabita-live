Playing the Magician

By Jayanta Mohapatra

Sunday, Rest day of the Week
when I turn the pages of the morning Newspaper
and bury the simple sadness of autumn
in the spilt blood of another child`s murder,
Somehow I do not think it`s a Story at all,
In the distance comes the dying echo
of a moving train, and you are suddenly someone
old and distant, a goat hanging in a butcher`s shop,
seeing more than you`ve seen before in your life.
The leaves susurrate in the bamboo stems
to make me sense that there was something
may be behind it that was quite important,
A persistence of my own mortality perhaps.
Or the oneness between me and the world.
Grass had closed its eyes as it watched
A Prime Minister pick up the body of India
Which he felt lay battered inside his chest.
Sunday when I thought I was accountable to myself,
Was I dying within myself for the sorrows -
I suffered for the world that wasn`t with me enough?
Sometimes the coils of ropes of a country`s conscience
Straighten themselves and rise up into the air.
Into God`s hand, hanging there unseen to help, Sometimes
Someone asks himself, before he falls asleep,
Whether he was happy to live in the land that was India.
Even chaos shuns the end,
A handful of space from the size
Of a cathedral. Although no wind is there still,
The boat tied offshore has moved a little,
And tied with it the apprehension of another self.
But the world is there, beginning to speak again,
And I`ve lost the ability to disappear.
Looks as if with all its silvery light,
the world cannot touch me
with its last sentence, bold, sinless.
Cumulus darkens things, flying past.
Which part of one is it that has to be put away?
The logic of winning, or the magic of living
backwards in time? I can`t resist the urge
that takes hold of me for some unspoken chaos,
or the pain of collecting mushrooms in the wet woods,
that never fail to remind us of who we think we are.
