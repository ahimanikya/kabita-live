LIFE AT THE TRAFFIC SIGNAL

By Sudeshna Mukherjee

She wakes at the crack of dawn
Her sleep deprived eyes looks momentarily disoriented
Through the gloam she tries to focus.

She goes about her morning duties
Harsh tones and routine words greet her
As the city nudges itself into wakefulness
Her childish eyes watch `others` in uniform going to school.

Her agile mind asks,;Is school like Aanganvaadis?;
A sigh escapes she would never know
A rude prod pokes into her thin frame
It doesn`t hurt her anymore.

Quickly she picks up the white `mogras`
Her mother has taught her how to weave them
`Venis` interspread with tiny rose buds
They are for the ladies who hurry to their respective jobs.

Traffic builds up
with armful of `gajras` and her youngest sibling straddling her
She waits for the signal to change to red
Then she weaves across the cars to sell her freshly made adornments.

Traffic moves and accelerates
She waits patiently for her situation to change
Her large honey brown eyes has a recurrent dream
Would she be able to achieve it?

She has dared to dream
An impossible dream
The Sun shines harshly upon her
Beads of sweat trickles down her frail body.

Raucous sound envelopes her
Her cracked heels gathers dust
On a half empty stomach
Her hope clings to her.

Days pass years pass they grow on her
Now she is a replica of her prematurely wizened mother
Her rough tone rings through
As she plies her stuff.

Grey undertones blanket grey pavements
Dreams have died a natural death
Dawn and dusk blend as one monotone.
