The Burden of Creation

By Gayatri Mavuru

I carry the dust of a thousand dawns,
fingers stained with pigments,
each hue a silent witness to the wars I`ve waged
against the blank canvas,
that pale, indifferent face staring back,
waiting to devour or reveal.

I carry the weight of failures I`ve etched and erased,
ghosts of half-born visions hovering
in the silence between breath and brushstroke,
in the raw, unhealed spaces of my hands.

In the nights where sleep dissolved like smoke,
I forged my path through dark labyrinths,
a pilgrim of lines and colours,
seeking a truth too vast to name.
I bled for beauty, gave my bones to light,
only to find myself lost,
in love with the loss.

I carry the laughter of those who never understood
this hunger, this wound that would not close,
and the quiet scorn of a world content
with surfaces, afraid of depth.

But I also carry the moment I first touched eternity,
when my hands moved as if on borrowed grace,
and something larger than breath and body
flowed through me, a river of fire,
burning away all that was small,
leaving only the vast silence of stars.

I carry the peace of knowing
that I am nothing, and I am everything,
the hand that shapes the clay,
the clay that shapes the hand,
a speck in the storm,
a drop returning to the boundless sea.

And when the earth gathers me back,
I will leave behind the bruised weight of the world,
all the struggle and striving, the ache of want
but I will carry the light I have gathered,
the stillness that remains
when creation and creator are one.

What I carry to my rest
is not sorrow, not triumph, but this
a quiet flame, a seed of wonder,
and the faint whisper of all that cannot die.
