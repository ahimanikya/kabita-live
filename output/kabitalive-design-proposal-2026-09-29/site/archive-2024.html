Story behind the cover · Issue 26

The warmth that stays

The fire has lowered its voice. A little warmth remains where the meal was made, and the room carries the scent of smoke and grain. Those who ate have moved on to other things. The hearth keeps the shape of their gathering a while longer, without asking anyone to return.

Cultural connection
The hearth and earthen kitchen express domestic memory; warmth is the subject, without idealising the burdens of cooking or fuel collection.

November 2024

Story behind the cover · Issue 25

Steps down to the day

The steps hold the coolness of the night. Beside the vessel, water moves past without knowing whose morning it will enter. A small daily journey pauses at the river's edge. Between carrying and being carried, between home and current, the day finds its first unhurried breath.

Cultural connection
River steps and water-carrying as an imagined everyday setting; no particular ghat or ritual is identified.

October 2024

Story behind the cover · Issue 24

One flower after another

The thread is almost invisible beneath the flowers. One by one, their separate brightness becomes something that can be offered, worn or placed beside a door. The making is quiet. What looks like abundance at the end began as a hand returning patiently to one small gesture.

Cultural connection
Flower-threading and everyday acts of welcome and offering; no specific festival or sacred obligation is asserted.

Issue 23

September 2024

Story behind the cover · Issue 23

The sky comes to the field

Water settles between the young rice plants and brings the sky close enough to touch. A cloud breaks into pieces when the wind passes. The field is both labour and reflection, both what must be tended and what cannot be held. For a moment, the two share the same light.

Cultural connection
Flooded paddy fields provide the Odisha agrarian reference; this is an imagined scene, not a record of a particular farm.

Story behind the cover · Issue 22

Someone will be back

The bicycle leans against the wall with the ease of a familiar visitor. Its front wheel is still turned towards the lane. Someone has gone inside for water, a conversation, a few minutes longer than intended. The image keeps their place without needing to show their face.

Cultural connection
A bicycle and a lived-in wall suggest everyday mobility and neighbourhood life, without turning poverty or age into decoration.

Story behind the cover · Issue 21

The leaf lets go

Water gathers at the edge of the leaf until its own weight becomes a decision. One drop falls, then the leaf is still again. Beneath this small departure, the garden continues. There are days when letting go looks less like a dramatic ending and more like making room for rain.

Cultural connection
A banana leaf after rainfall; a close natural study linked to gardens and familiar household landscapes.

June 2024

Story behind the cover · Issue 20

The swing waits for laughter

The ropes have kept the curve of someone's afternoon. Now the swing is still, and the branch holds its weight as gently as before. A little space remains between earth and sky, ready for a foot to leave the ground. Joy sometimes survives as an invitation rather than an event.

Cultural connection
A village swing and remembered play.

May 2024

Story behind the cover · Issue 19

The tree's patient gift

The fruit has grown quietly while the days were occupied with other things. Now its rough green surface catches a little light beneath the leaves. What takes time does not always announce its labour. Some gifts arrive with the whole season still inside them, waiting for a household to gather.

Cultural connection
Jackfruit in a home landscape; an original reflection on growth and shared food, without assigning a particular recipe or ritual.

Story behind the cover · Issue 18

A breeze made by hand

The fan rests where a hand has left it. Its woven surface remembers a rhythm too small to be called work, yet large enough to soften a difficult afternoon. Outside, the heat continues. Inside, a little moving air becomes a kindness that needs no explanation and leaves no trace.

Cultural connection
A palm-leaf hand fan and everyday craft; the imagined object is not attributed to a named maker or village.

Story behind the cover · Issue 17

A forest breathing softly

The forest does not need a grand entrance. A fern uncurls beside water; a small blossom finds space on a dark trunk. Further in, the trees gather their own weather. To look closely here is to discover that wonder often begins below the level at which we usually search for it.

Cultural connection
Similipal's forests, streams, orchids and ferns ground this imagined ecological study.

Story behind the cover · Issue 16

A language looks back

A face looks out from the quiet of the page. Beyond it are lives too ordinary to become monuments, yet too full to be left unwritten. This tribute imagines literature as an act of attention: a language turning towards its own people, and finding that their everyday voices can carry a world.

Cultural connection
An interpretive tribute to Fakir Mohan Senapati and his attention to common people in Odia prose.

January 2024

Story behind the cover · Issue 15

What the hands have gathered

The sheaf is small enough to hold and large enough to contain months. Rain, waiting, bent backs and uncertain skies have arrived together in these stems. The field will soon look different. For a moment, the harvest can be seen as a gathering of time, not simply a gathering of grain.

Cultural connection
Rice harvest and agricultural labour; gratitude does not erase the work or uncertainty that produced it.
