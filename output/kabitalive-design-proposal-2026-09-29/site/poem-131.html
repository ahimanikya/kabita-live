What No One Told Me

By Urna Bose

No one told me, the rotis wouldn`t
glisten with ghee anymore,
from the open Milton casserole,
the roundish nourishing sun,
of our roundish dining table.

No one told me,neither would they be
that soft, fluffy, and comforting.
Atta kneaded with water,
half a teaspoon patience,
and a cupful of love.

No one told me, the ghee wouldn`t
smell homemadeanymore.
Smartly bought on my
smartphone`s over-smart app,
from a new-fangled fancy organic brand.
With a smiling pink cow for its logo,
at a super-saver, super-smart 30% discount.

No one told me, the need to eat
would remain, but it`s not the same
as a belligerent hunger,
rising up from a savage intestine,
worked up by the
sukhi laal mirchi tadka given to
fresh masoor dal.

So many people hadso many things to say.
People always have, so much to say.
Yet no one told me this.

No one told me, the dining table would
never have that once white but
yellowish now, crochet tablecloth,
demurely yet cleverly covering
its slightly apologetic, chipped off edges.

No one told me, the kitchen would stop
smelling of you,
and that the sink would stop shining,
your straggly, overworked Scotch-Brite
punished to a corner,
where grief and soot hang heavy.

No one told me, dinner would become
a silent chore, without
the dutiful background score
of your chitter-chatter.

So many people hadso many things to say.
“Be strong.”
“Listen, time heals everything.”
“Don`t hold the tears in, ok?”
People always have so much to say.

Yet not a single person told me this.
That I would stop loving
ghee-soaked rotis,
once you`re gone Ma.

Not a single person told me,
that the things
you once loved so dearly,
you can start hating too.
