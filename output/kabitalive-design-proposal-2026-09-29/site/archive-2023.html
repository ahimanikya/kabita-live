Story behind the cover · Issue 14

The chair keeps a little warmth

A shawl remains on the chair after the conversation has ended. Its folds hold the suggestion of shoulders, of someone who leaned forward to listen. Nothing dramatic has happened. Yet the room feels inhabited by more than furniture, and the afternoon has become difficult to call empty.

Cultural connection
Domestic cloth, winter light and remembered companionship; an imagined home, not a named person's belongings.

November 2023

Story behind the cover · Issue 13

A second moon

The moon has crossed a great distance to tremble beside the reeds. Its reflection will not stay still long enough to be kept. Perhaps that is why the water feels less lonely tonight: something far away has come near, without needing to stop being far away.

Cultural connection
An imagined Odisha lagoon at night; the emotional story concerns distance and companionship.

October 2023

Story behind the cover · Issue 12

The morning gathers quietly

The flowers have fallen before the house wakes. Small pale stars lie close to the earth, their orange centres holding the last warmth of night. Someone may gather them, or the breeze may move them first. Beauty has arrived without requiring that anybody be ready to receive it.

Cultural connection
Night-flowering jasmine provides the floral starting point; the image does not claim a particular local devotional practice.

September 2023

Story behind the cover · Issue 11

The rain can stay outside

Under the thatch, the rain becomes something to listen to. The veranda is neither fully indoors nor fully in the world, and that is its particular gift. A person can sit here without leaving home or closing the day away. The roof makes a small, generous pause in the weather.

Cultural connection
A thatched veranda evokes vernacular domestic shelter.

Story behind the cover · Issue 10

The hour the stone remembers

Light reaches the carved wheel and moves across it without turning a spoke. The stone has learned to hold time differently: in worn edges, patient shadows, a blade of grass finding its place. A visitor passes. The hour continues, leaving almost nothing and changing everything it touches.

Cultural connection
The carved chariot wheels of the Sun Temple at Konark are the reference.

Story behind the cover · Issue 9

The river takes its time

The river bends where a straight line would have been easier to draw. Along the bank, land and water have been answering each other for longer than the path remembers. There is no hurry in the curve. It is a reminder that arriving can include the grace of going around.

Cultural connection
An imagined Odisha river landscape; no precise river, village or conservation condition is asserted.

June 2023

Story behind the cover · Issue 8

A small place to begin

The seedling has only a little soil, but it begins anyway. Beside its first leaves, the old bowl takes on a new purpose. Care does not always need a wide field. Sometimes it is the decision to give one small living thing a place, and to return tomorrow with water.

Cultural connection
An everyday act of home growing and reuse, offered as a contemporary narrative rather than a named heritage practice.

May 2023

Story behind the cover · Issue 7

The shade knows everyone

The tree has heard conversations that no one remembers in full. People have waited beneath it, disagreed, rested, gone away. Its shade remains available without asking for an introduction. Some places become part of a community through the simple, repeated act of making room.

Cultural connection
A tamarind tree as a shared village landscape; the story is imagined and does not document a particular meeting place.

April 2023

Story behind the cover · Issue 6

A little sky indoors

Water rests inside the bowl as though it has forgotten the journey that brought it here. A flower touches the surface. In this small circle, the room acquires another kind of space: not larger, simply deeper. There are poems that ask no more of us than to look and remain.

Cultural connection
A domestic still life of water, metal and flower; no prescribed religious symbolism is assigned.

Story behind the cover · Issue 5

Where the hills exhale

Cloud moves across the grass and the ridge changes its mind about colour. Beyond it, another hill loosens into blue. There is no single point where the view becomes complete. The path asks only for the next few steps, and the wind makes enough room for whatever we brought with us.

Cultural connection
Deomali in Koraput, within the Eastern Ghats, is the geographical reference.

Story behind the cover · Issue 4

A world held in a thread

Silver bends into a shape that seems too light to stay. Between its fine lines, empty space becomes part of the ornament. The maker has worked with both what is present and what is left open. A poem can be built this way too: with precision, and with room for breath.

Cultural connection
Cuttack's tarakasi, or silver filigree, provides the craft reference.

January 2023

Story behind the cover · Issue 3

The sea, held close

The shell lies where the water has left it, small against the long patience of the shore. Someone may lift it and bring it close to an ear. Whether the sound is memory or sea hardly matters in that moment. Listening has already made a distance feel smaller.

Cultural connection
A shore-found shell as a poetic object; this image makes no claim about a species, collection practice or ritual conch.
