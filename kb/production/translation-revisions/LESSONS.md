# Lessons retained from the revision batches

These are contextual checks from AI self-review, not universal replacement rules or independent language certification.

- **Preserve an intentional image even when it is unusual.** In poem 810, the shade of falling raindrops is already in the Hindi original. Removing it for ordinary English would lose the poem’s image. Both existing targets were retained.
- **Check the same kinship term against each source.** Poem 812 explicitly says दादी, supporting the Odia paternal-grandmother term. Poem 821 only says grandmother, leaving its lineage open. Do not apply a blanket rule to either term.
- **Separate an unfamiliar literary register from an accidental one.** Poem 809’s lost liveliness belongs to a plain account of childhood grief; quickening adds an avoidable association. Poem 811’s elaborate devotional voice is already present in its English source and should remain elaborate.
- **Use source roles before remembered cultural identities.** Poem 811 names Jara as hunter. The Odia addition of a community identity was removed without replacing the figure or explaining the mythology.
- **Do not infer friends’ gender from an addressee’s presentation.** Poem 817’s friends can become दोस्तों without fixing the narrator’s or addressee’s gender. The broader Hindi voice choices remain separate source questions.
- **Distinguish a clear local repair from an unresolved whole passage.** In poem 813, mould can be distinguished from mushrooms while the compressed buried-footsteps image remains provisional. Do not claim the whole translation is resolved because one noun is corrected.
- **Relineation needs a target boundary check.** Poem 823’s isolated English preposition was joined to its phrase, with thirteen lines and explicit stanza positions. Source-index trimming must not decide a translated poem’s last line.

Evidence: [batch-002 review](batch-002/review.json), [batch-003 review](batch-003/review.json), and the preserved [pilot comparison](../../artifacts/review/translation-pilot-2026-10-04/COMPARISON.md). Open questions remain in the batch records.

- **Let target syntax place the line break.** Poems791 and793 exposed English postmodifiers copied after Hindi/Odia nouns. Reordering the phrase can improve breath without shortening the poem; even matching line counts receive explicit target mappings.
- **Treat an expanded title as an editorial decision.** Poem801’s source noun did not warrant explaining the addressee in the title. Both titles are now shorter; uncertain damaged body text remains held.
- **Do not neutralize erotic language through abstraction.** Poem796 uses bodily love-making and a generative double meaning. The revision restores the physical register without adding a sexual act; the compact seed rendering still carries a documented tradeoff.

Evidence for these additions: batch005–007 full-reading snapshots and reviews. These remain self-review findings.

- **Read negation as a relationship, not isolated words.** Poem 781 explicitly contrasts release with moksha; changing not/rather into not only/also erased that distinction. Preserve the distinction without pretending its philosophical relation is settled.
- **A target need not imitate apparent source wrapping.** Poem 776’s eight movements became eight target quatrains. Full coverage, explicit 32-line mappings and browser checks protect the final stanza; the new stanza form is an editorial target choice, not newly authenticated source evidence.
- **Preserve what remains inside a claim.** Poem 785’s Odia version needed ଯେ to keep the speaker’s alteration of a story within the addressee’s accusation. Natural cadence must not turn reported claims into established facts.
- **Clarify an antecedent without overstating the prior error.** Poem 787’s fire is called Kali before the woman is later named Draupadi/Durga. Explicit fire in each target protects that distinction; existing ସେ/वह could also have referred to fire, so this is recorded as a clarity improvement rather than a proven universal misreading.

Evidence: batches 010–012 current full readings and exact drafts. These lessons remain contextual self-review, with source questions and independent review pending.

- **A cognate can hide an image change.** In poem 763, ବାସେ carries scent within a poem framed by an earth-scented girl. Hindi बसते and English dwell had replaced smell with residence; the local repair restores the sensory recurrence. The possible bird/season reading elsewhere remains a separate hold.
- **Check the verb’s object before smoothing an image.** Poem 771’s voices tangle the evening air; making them become tangled in it reverses the relation. The unusual agency is part of the poem.
- **Mood can differ across languages even when surface wording looks similar.** Poem 764’s Hindi करो turned a statement into a request. Odia ସଜାଅ can also serve the source’s present-tense address, so that version was retained. Do not copy a repair mechanically across targets.
- **Do not neutralize an uncomfortable metaphor.** Poem 766 says a tiger hunts a tigress. The revisions restore hunting rather than neutral searching, without adding a sexual act or resolving the poem’s damaged wording.

## Edition 40 — batches 024–026

- Do not add a lyric agent to an impersonal source. Poem720's flowers went unpicked; the English draft had made the speaker explicitly unable to pick them. Other first-person actions later in the poem do not settle that opening.
- Preserve the direction of contact. Soap721 ends with refusing the soap access to bodily stains; a Hindi word-order repair prevents the stains from becoming the apparent agents touching the soap. Its cupboard shelf also should not become a layer.
- Match the intensity of an action. In709, sneaking up on someone is not yet seizing them, and falling from a train does not itself specify a moving train. Removing those additions preserves the disturbing literary rhetoric without amplifying it.
- A recognizable cultural name and an idiomatic phrase need different treatment. Jack Frost710 keeps the full name with its brief frost cue, while Fanny Adams remains the existing idiomatic nothing; it should not acquire a new person in the story.
- Check possibility as well as action. The envisioned river717 is where birds might bathe and worshippers might find grace; dropping the modal makes the image more certain. The poem’s devotional and polemical stance remains the speaker’s.
- Separate saved preparation from applied coverage. Exact review snapshots are useful before integration, but unchanged proposed targets must not inflate the completed queue, and unapplied changes must not appear as stale catalogue edits. The queue now requires an integration receipt; regression tests cover these cases.
