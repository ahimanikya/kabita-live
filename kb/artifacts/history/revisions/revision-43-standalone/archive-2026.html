Story behind the cover · Issue 47

After the rain

A flower has fallen, but its fragrance has not finished travelling. Rain gathers the red earth into a small dark mirror. Nothing announces a beginning here; the courtyard simply opens its eyes again. A poem can arrive in just this way, after we have stopped waiting for it.

Cultural connection
Everyday Odisha: rain, earthen courtyards and the sensory memory of home.

Story behind the cover · Issue 46

The colour before the story

Before the story has a face, a hand gives it a border. A brush pauses above the cloth, holding a little red earth in its tip. Outside, the village carries on. Inside this small interval, patience becomes colour, and colour prepares a place for someone else's wondering.

Cultural connection
Raghurajpur is known for pattachitra, a painting tradition on prepared cloth.

Story behind the cover · Issue 45

Evening on the water

The boat has brought everyone home and is alone at last. A loose rope touches the water; the reeds make room for the evening. What remains after a day's work is not emptiness. It is the quiet in which the lake, and the person who crossed it, can breathe.

Cultural connection
Odisha's lagoon landscapes and fishing livelihoods; an imagined waterside scene, not an identified boat or community.

Story behind the cover · Issue 44

A threshold in rain

Someone drew a welcome before the clouds arrived. Now water softens the white lines, carrying a little of the doorway into the earth. The welcome survives its own fading. Tomorrow a hand may draw it again, because some forms of care are made to be renewed.

Cultural connection
Jhoti and chita provide the visual reference: restrained white threshold drawing within an imagined earthen home.

Story behind the cover · Issue 43

Room for water

The vessels wait without knowing what they will hold. One may carry drinking water, another a flower, another only the coolness of a shaded room. Clay has already learned the shape of receiving. The potter's hands are absent, but their patience remains in every rounded shoulder.

Cultural connection
Everyday earthenware and the labour of pottery; no specific craft cluster is claimed for this imagined scene.

Story behind the cover · Issue 42

The wind reads first

At the edge of the sea, the trees never finish their sentence. Wind moves through the thin leaves, carrying salt further inland. A person could stand here with nothing to say and still be understood. The shore listens with its whole length, then gives the silence back.

Cultural connection
Odisha's coastal landscape, interpreted through casuarina, sand and sea air.

Story behind the cover · Issue 41

A small red insistence

One branch offers its colour to an otherwise quiet day. The flowers do not ask the whole landscape to change; they simply burn where they are. In their small red insistence lives a familiar courage: to keep a little warmth visible, even when the world is looking elsewhere.

Cultural connection
Palash provides the botanical and seasonal starting point; the emotional reading is original.

Story behind the cover · Issue 40

The loom remembers a voice

A thread crosses another, then another, until the empty space can be held in the hands. The loom makes a rhythm without raising its voice. This imagined tribute places poetry beside patient work: a life finding music in repeated gestures, and a language finding strength in its own people.

Cultural connection
An imaginative homage to Gangadhar Meher, the Odia poet born into a weaver family in Barpali.

January 2026

Story behind the cover · Issue 39

Before the field wakes

Morning arrives quietly enough to hear the field breathing. The grain bends under its own ripeness while mist hides the far boundary. Someone will soon come along the path. For now, the harvest belongs to that brief hour between work remembered and work about to begin.

Cultural connection
Rice cultivation and rural mornings in Odisha; an imagined field, without assigning it to a named village.
