# A quiet place for image and word

The signature reads **ମାଟିର ମହକ · ମନର ସ୍ୱର** on one line. In a stacked composition, set the two phrases on separate lines without punctuation. This is a brand styling choice. Original poem punctuation remains unchanged.

Use the existing Odisha-inspired watercolor as the site's recurring illustration: to the right of internal page introductions, in writer-page openings, and as a right-aligned footer echo. The English masthead and its 17 px navigation remain unchanged; language filters remain beside poem listings. Issue-opening pages use the issue's own cover on the right. On mobile, artwork becomes a small accent and text wraps naturally.

Poems may have one individual illustration beside their title. The three local poem readers now demonstrate that approach; the verse stays on clear warm paper. Contemporary watercolor/gouache and everyday regional materials provide the modern/heritage balance without claiming an authentic traditional art form. These are proposed interpretations of the available excerpts and need editorial review against each full poem before publication.

## Artwork and provenance

All three new illustrations were made with the built-in image_gen tool. Exact prompts are in PROMPTS.json. PNG originals and optimized WebP copies are in site/assets/poem-art/. Existing site and monthly-cover art is preserved.

- dokana: a closed shop after rain; indigo timber, red earth and a padlock.
- kshanika: an empty rain-washed platform and bench.
- drink: an earthen cup at an evening window; a metaphorical atmosphere, without alcohol imagery.

Use only one image per poem opening. Do not add decorative images between stanzas, place text over photographs, fabricate documentary locations or treat the artwork as the author's endorsed interpretation. Published work should carry a verified artist or generated-art credit.
