# Kabita Live · Forty-seven stories of home

Original narratives for newly proposed cover art; these are not historical issue summaries.

## Issue 47 · September 2026 — After the rain

**Weather / renewal**

A flower has fallen, but its fragrance has not finished travelling. Rain gathers the red earth into a small dark mirror. Nothing announces a beginning here; the courtyard simply opens its eyes again. A poem can arrive in just this way, after we have stopped waiting for it.

Cultural connection: Everyday Odisha: rain, earthen courtyards and the sensory memory of home.

## Issue 46 · August 2026 — The colour before the story

**Craft / attention**

Before the story has a face, a hand gives it a border. A brush pauses above the cloth, holding a little red earth in its tip. Outside, the village carries on. Inside this small interval, patience becomes colour, and colour prepares a place for someone else's wondering.

Cultural connection: Raghurajpur is known for pattachitra, a painting tradition on prepared cloth. This is an imagined workshop and an original border study.

Reference: [Odisha Tourism · Raghurajpur](https://odishatourism.gov.in/content/tourism/en/discover/attractions/arts-crafts/raghurajpur.html)

## Issue 45 · July 2026 — Evening on the water

**Water / rest**

The boat has brought everyone home and is alone at last. A loose rope touches the water; the reeds make room for the evening. What remains after a day's work is not emptiness. It is the quiet in which the lake, and the person who crossed it, can breathe.

Cultural connection: Odisha's lagoon landscapes and fishing livelihoods; an imagined waterside scene, not an identified boat or community.

## Issue 44 · June 2026 — A threshold in rain

**Home / welcome**

Someone drew a welcome before the clouds arrived. Now water softens the white lines, carrying a little of the doorway into the earth. The welcome survives its own fading. Tomorrow a hand may draw it again, because some forms of care are made to be renewed.

Cultural connection: Jhoti and chita provide the visual reference: restrained white threshold drawing within an imagined earthen home.

Reference: [Government of Odisha · Jhoti, Chita, Muruja](https://odisha.gov.in/en/odisha-tourism/jhoti-chita-muruja)

## Issue 43 · May 2026 — Room for water

**Craft / possibility**

The vessels wait without knowing what they will hold. One may carry drinking water, another a flower, another only the coolness of a shaded room. Clay has already learned the shape of receiving. The potter's hands are absent, but their patience remains in every rounded shoulder.

Cultural connection: Everyday earthenware and the labour of pottery; no specific craft cluster is claimed for this imagined scene.

## Issue 42 · April 2026 — The wind reads first

**Coast / listening**

At the edge of the sea, the trees never finish their sentence. Wind moves through the thin leaves, carrying salt further inland. A person could stand here with nothing to say and still be understood. The shore listens with its whole length, then gives the silence back.

Cultural connection: Odisha's coastal landscape, interpreted through casuarina, sand and sea air.

## Issue 41 · March 2026 — A small red insistence

**Flora / resilience**

One branch offers its colour to an otherwise quiet day. The flowers do not ask the whole landscape to change; they simply burn where they are. In their small red insistence lives a familiar courage: to keep a little warmth visible, even when the world is looking elsewhere.

Cultural connection: Palash provides the botanical and seasonal starting point; the emotional reading is original.

## Issue 40 · February 2026 — The loom remembers a voice

**Literature / labour**

A thread crosses another, then another, until the empty space can be held in the hands. The loom makes a rhythm without raising its voice. This imagined tribute places poetry beside patient work: a life finding music in repeated gestures, and a language finding strength in its own people.

Cultural connection: An imaginative homage to Gangadhar Meher, the Odia poet born into a weaver family in Barpali. This is not his documented loom or workshop.

Reference: [Sambalpur district · Eminent personalities](https://sambalpur.odisha.gov.in/en/about-district/eminent-personalities)

## Issue 39 · January 2026 — Before the field wakes

**Agrarian / stillness**

Morning arrives quietly enough to hear the field breathing. The grain bends under its own ripeness while mist hides the far boundary. Someone will soon come along the path. For now, the harvest belongs to that brief hour between work remembered and work about to begin.

Cultural connection: Rice cultivation and rural mornings in Odisha; an imagined field, without assigning it to a named village.

## Issue 38 · December 2025 — Enough light for one room

**Home / tenderness**

The lamp does not try to illuminate the entire night. It keeps one small circle warm: a wall, a pair of waiting hands, the place where a story might be told. Outside, darkness continues. Inside, there is enough light for someone to feel that they have been expected.

Cultural connection: An everyday earthen oil lamp, approached through intimacy rather than a claim about a particular ceremony.

## Issue 37 · November 2025 — A boat for remembering

**Living culture / passage**

A small boat leaves a large wish behind it. On the water, paper becomes a traveller for a moment, carrying what the hands could not keep. The shore is still close, yet departure has already begun. Memory sometimes needs only a little current to find its way forward.

Cultural connection: A poetic reference to Kartika boat-floating traditions in Odisha; an imagined contemporary paper boat, not a reconstruction of an ancient vessel.

Reference: [Odisha Tourism · Festivals](https://odishatourism.gov.in/content/tourism/en/experience/themes/festivals.html)

## Issue 36 · October 2025 — The season in white

**Season / change**

Along the path, pale plumes catch the light that the day is slowly losing. No calendar hangs in this field, yet the grasses know something has changed. They lean together without agreeing on a direction. The season passes through them like news spoken very gently.

Cultural connection: Kans grass and the changing seasonal landscape; no single festival is asserted by the image.

## Issue 35 · September 2025 — Not yet a flower

**Water / anticipation**

The bud keeps its colour folded close. Around it, water holds an imperfect sky and the broad leaves of yesterday. There is no need to hurry the opening. Some poems, like some affections, become themselves by remaining quiet until the right light finds them.

Cultural connection: Lotus and pond life, familiar across Odisha and beyond; the narrative concerns anticipation rather than ritual symbolism.

## Issue 34 · August 2025 — A little sky, stitched

**Craft / invention**

A needle carries the sun across a piece of cloth. Nearby, a bird waits for its wing and a flower for its last small curve. Under the maker's hands, scraps find a sky together. What was separate becomes shelter: a bright, patient answer to the day's ordinary shade.

Cultural connection: Pipili is associated with chandua and appliqué. The image studies sewn shapes and raised cloth edges in an imagined work in progress.

Reference: [Odisha Tourism · Glimpses of Odisha](https://odishatourism.gov.in/content/tourism/en/discover/about-odisha/glimpses-of-odisha.html)

## Issue 33 · July 2025 — The bowl holds a note

**Craft / resonance**

The hammer rests, but the bowl seems to remember its sound. Light follows the faint circles left by the maker, moving slowly towards the empty centre. Soon there may be a meal here. For a moment, the vessel holds something less visible: the rhythm by which it came into being.

Cultural connection: Kantilo has a brass and bell-metal craft tradition. This concept specifically concerns kansa, bell-metal, rather than treating all yellow metals as interchangeable.

Reference: [Nayagarh district · Kantilo](https://nayagarh.odisha.gov.in/en/tourism/tourist-places/kantilo-nilamadhab)

## Issue 32 · June 2025 — When the lane turns fragrant

**Weather / arrival**

The first rain reaches the lane before anyone steps outside. Dust darkens; the air becomes familiar in a way no photograph can hold. A door remains half open. Somewhere within, a sentence is left unfinished because everyone has recognised the same fragrance at once.

Cultural connection: The scent of rain on earth anchors the brand's ମାଟିର ମହକ theme through everyday experience.

## Issue 31 · May 2025 — Summer, carried home

**Food / abundance**

The basket is heavier on the return journey. Beneath the leaves, summer has gathered its sweetness into uneven yellow shapes. At home, someone will choose the ripest one for somebody else. A season becomes a memory through such small arrangements of generosity, long before anyone calls them poetry.

Cultural connection: Mango season and the everyday culture of bringing food home; no named cultivar or local origin is claimed.

## Issue 30 · April 2025 — Salt in the cotton

**Textile / everyday life**

The gamucha dries slowly in the sea air. It has known shoulders, water, a hurried hand; now the breeze lifts one corner as though turning a page. Nothing precious has been put away. The day's tenderness hangs here in plain sight, woven into an object used and used again.

Cultural connection: Puri gamucha is the user's everyday cultural reference. This imagined cotton cloth is not presented as a certified weave, temple-issued cloth or prescribed ritual garment.

## Issue 29 · March 2025 — What the woodland holds

**Ecology / belonging**

The path is scarcely wider than a thought. Kendu leaves hold their quiet above it while last season's fallen leaves soften every step. The woodland is neither empty nor waiting to be discovered. It carries its own daily life, and the lives of people who already know its shade.

Cultural connection: Kendu woodland generally, as clarified by the user. Kendu leaves are an important forest produce and livelihood connection in Odisha; no particular forest or community is depicted.

Reference: [Odisha Kendu Leaves Organisation](https://kenduleaves.odisha.gov.in/)

## Issue 28 · February 2025 — The sari keeps the river

**Textile / memory**

A sari rests in folds, but its patterns are still travelling. Along the border, colour meets colour like a river meeting another season. Someone will lift it soon, and the room will lose this small landscape. The cloth will carry it elsewhere, close to the warmth of a living body.

Cultural connection: Sambalpuri bandha is a tie-and-dye weaving tradition. This is generated textile interpretation, not an authenticated artisan sample or evidence of a particular motif's meaning.

Reference: [Sambalpur district · Handicraft](https://sambalpur.odisha.gov.in/en/tourism/handicraft)

## Issue 27 · January 2025 — The net before morning

**Livelihood / preparation**

The net lies gathered beside the boat, holding only the light for now. Every knot belongs to a task repeated so often that the hands can remember it without looking. The water has made no promises. Still, preparation goes on: an ordinary form of hope with work inside it.

Cultural connection: Coastal fishing work provides the setting; the scene avoids assigning a livelihood or identity to an invented named community.

## Issue 26 · December 2024 — The warmth that stays

**Home / continuity**

The fire has lowered its voice. A little warmth remains where the meal was made, and the room carries the scent of smoke and grain. Those who ate have moved on to other things. The hearth keeps the shape of their gathering a while longer, without asking anyone to return.

Cultural connection: The hearth and earthen kitchen express domestic memory; warmth is the subject, without idealising the burdens of cooking or fuel collection.

## Issue 25 · November 2024 — Steps down to the day

**River / routine**

The steps hold the coolness of the night. Beside the vessel, water moves past without knowing whose morning it will enter. A small daily journey pauses at the river's edge. Between carrying and being carried, between home and current, the day finds its first unhurried breath.

Cultural connection: River steps and water-carrying as an imagined everyday setting; no particular ghat or ritual is identified.

## Issue 24 · October 2024 — One flower after another

**Living culture / care**

The thread is almost invisible beneath the flowers. One by one, their separate brightness becomes something that can be offered, worn or placed beside a door. The making is quiet. What looks like abundance at the end began as a hand returning patiently to one small gesture.

Cultural connection: Flower-threading and everyday acts of welcome and offering; no specific festival or sacred obligation is asserted.

## Issue 23 · September 2024 — The sky comes to the field

**Agrarian / reflection**

Water settles between the young rice plants and brings the sky close enough to touch. A cloud breaks into pieces when the wind passes. The field is both labour and reflection, both what must be tended and what cannot be held. For a moment, the two share the same light.

Cultural connection: Flooded paddy fields provide the Odisha agrarian reference; this is an imagined scene, not a record of a particular farm.

## Issue 22 · August 2024 — Someone will be back

**Everyday life / absence**

The bicycle leans against the wall with the ease of a familiar visitor. Its front wheel is still turned towards the lane. Someone has gone inside for water, a conversation, a few minutes longer than intended. The image keeps their place without needing to show their face.

Cultural connection: A bicycle and a lived-in wall suggest everyday mobility and neighbourhood life, without turning poverty or age into decoration.

## Issue 21 · July 2024 — The leaf lets go

**Flora / release**

Water gathers at the edge of the leaf until its own weight becomes a decision. One drop falls, then the leaf is still again. Beneath this small departure, the garden continues. There are days when letting go looks less like a dramatic ending and more like making room for rain.

Cultural connection: A banana leaf after rainfall; a close natural study linked to gardens and familiar household landscapes.

## Issue 20 · June 2024 — The swing waits for laughter

**Living culture / play**

The ropes have kept the curve of someone's afternoon. Now the swing is still, and the branch holds its weight as gently as before. A little space remains between earth and sky, ready for a foot to leave the ground. Joy sometimes survives as an invitation rather than an event.

Cultural connection: A village swing and remembered play. It can suggest Odisha's seasonal celebrations without claiming that the depicted scene documents a specific festival.

## Issue 19 · May 2024 — The tree's patient gift

**Food / growth**

The fruit has grown quietly while the days were occupied with other things. Now its rough green surface catches a little light beneath the leaves. What takes time does not always announce its labour. Some gifts arrive with the whole season still inside them, waiting for a household to gather.

Cultural connection: Jackfruit in a home landscape; an original reflection on growth and shared food, without assigning a particular recipe or ritual.

## Issue 18 · April 2024 — A breeze made by hand

**Craft / relief**

The fan rests where a hand has left it. Its woven surface remembers a rhythm too small to be called work, yet large enough to soften a difficult afternoon. Outside, the heat continues. Inside, a little moving air becomes a kindness that needs no explanation and leaves no trace.

Cultural connection: A palm-leaf hand fan and everyday craft; the imagined object is not attributed to a named maker or village.

## Issue 17 · March 2024 — A forest breathing softly

**Place / ecology**

The forest does not need a grand entrance. A fern uncurls beside water; a small blossom finds space on a dark trunk. Further in, the trees gather their own weather. To look closely here is to discover that wonder often begins below the level at which we usually search for it.

Cultural connection: Similipal's forests, streams, orchids and ferns ground this imagined ecological study. It is not a documentary photograph of a surveyed spot or a verified orchid species.

Reference: [Similipal · Attractions](https://similipal.org/pages/attractions)

## Issue 16 · February 2024 — A language looks back

**Literature / witness**

A face looks out from the quiet of the page. Beyond it are lives too ordinary to become monuments, yet too full to be left unwritten. This tribute imagines literature as an act of attention: a language turning towards its own people, and finding that their everyday voices can carry a world.

Cultural connection: An interpretive tribute to Fakir Mohan Senapati and his attention to common people in Odia prose. The narrative is original, not a quotation, historical scene or claim about this edition's contents.

Reference: [Government of Odisha · Fakir Mohan Senapati](https://odisha.gov.in/en/odisha-profile/eminent-personalities/fakir-mohan-senapati)

## Issue 15 · January 2024 — What the hands have gathered

**Agrarian / gratitude**

The sheaf is small enough to hold and large enough to contain months. Rain, waiting, bent backs and uncertain skies have arrived together in these stems. The field will soon look different. For a moment, the harvest can be seen as a gathering of time, not simply a gathering of grain.

Cultural connection: Rice harvest and agricultural labour; gratitude does not erase the work or uncertainty that produced it.

## Issue 14 · December 2023 — The chair keeps a little warmth

**Home / companionship**

A shawl remains on the chair after the conversation has ended. Its folds hold the suggestion of shoulders, of someone who leaned forward to listen. Nothing dramatic has happened. Yet the room feels inhabited by more than furniture, and the afternoon has become difficult to call empty.

Cultural connection: Domestic cloth, winter light and remembered companionship; an imagined home, not a named person's belongings.

## Issue 13 · November 2023 — A second moon

**Water / distance**

The moon has crossed a great distance to tremble beside the reeds. Its reflection will not stay still long enough to be kept. Perhaps that is why the water feels less lonely tonight: something far away has come near, without needing to stop being far away.

Cultural connection: An imagined Odisha lagoon at night; the emotional story concerns distance and companionship.

## Issue 12 · October 2023 — The morning gathers quietly

**Flora / fleeting beauty**

The flowers have fallen before the house wakes. Small pale stars lie close to the earth, their orange centres holding the last warmth of night. Someone may gather them, or the breeze may move them first. Beauty has arrived without requiring that anybody be ready to receive it.

Cultural connection: Night-flowering jasmine provides the floral starting point; the image does not claim a particular local devotional practice.

## Issue 11 · September 2023 — The rain can stay outside

**Architecture / shelter**

Under the thatch, the rain becomes something to listen to. The veranda is neither fully indoors nor fully in the world, and that is its particular gift. A person can sit here without leaving home or closing the day away. The roof makes a small, generous pause in the weather.

Cultural connection: A thatched veranda evokes vernacular domestic shelter. This is an imagined setting, not an architectural record of a named community's house.

## Issue 10 · August 2023 — The hour the stone remembers

**Heritage / time**

Light reaches the carved wheel and moves across it without turning a spoke. The stone has learned to hold time differently: in worn edges, patient shadows, a blade of grass finding its place. A visitor passes. The hour continues, leaving almost nothing and changing everything it touches.

Cultural connection: The carved chariot wheels of the Sun Temple at Konark are the reference. The generated close study is interpretive, not measured archaeological documentation.

Reference: [UNESCO · Sun Temple, Konark](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/246/)

## Issue 9 · July 2023 — The river takes its time

**Landscape / change**

The river bends where a straight line would have been easier to draw. Along the bank, land and water have been answering each other for longer than the path remembers. There is no hurry in the curve. It is a reminder that arriving can include the grace of going around.

Cultural connection: An imagined Odisha river landscape; no precise river, village or conservation condition is asserted.

## Issue 8 · June 2023 — A small place to begin

**Growth / nurture**

The seedling has only a little soil, but it begins anyway. Beside its first leaves, the old bowl takes on a new purpose. Care does not always need a wide field. Sometimes it is the decision to give one small living thing a place, and to return tomorrow with water.

Cultural connection: An everyday act of home growing and reuse, offered as a contemporary narrative rather than a named heritage practice.

## Issue 7 · May 2023 — The shade knows everyone

**Tree / community**

The tree has heard conversations that no one remembers in full. People have waited beneath it, disagreed, rested, gone away. Its shade remains available without asking for an introduction. Some places become part of a community through the simple, repeated act of making room.

Cultural connection: A tamarind tree as a shared village landscape; the story is imagined and does not document a particular meeting place.

## Issue 6 · April 2023 — A little sky indoors

**Home / contemplation**

Water rests inside the bowl as though it has forgotten the journey that brought it here. A flower touches the surface. In this small circle, the room acquires another kind of space: not larger, simply deeper. There are poems that ask no more of us than to look and remain.

Cultural connection: A domestic still life of water, metal and flower; no prescribed religious symbolism is assigned.

## Issue 5 · March 2023 — Where the hills exhale

**Place / openness**

Cloud moves across the grass and the ridge changes its mind about colour. Beyond it, another hill loosens into blue. There is no single point where the view becomes complete. The path asks only for the next few steps, and the wind makes enough room for whatever we brought with us.

Cultural connection: Deomali in Koraput, within the Eastern Ghats, is the geographical reference. This generated landscape is an artistic interpretation, not a location photograph.

Reference: [Koraput district · Deomali](https://koraput.odisha.gov.in/en/tourism/tourist-places/deomali)

## Issue 4 · February 2023 — A world held in a thread

**Craft / delicacy**

Silver bends into a shape that seems too light to stay. Between its fine lines, empty space becomes part of the ornament. The maker has worked with both what is present and what is left open. A poem can be built this way too: with precision, and with room for breath.

Cultural connection: Cuttack's tarakasi, or silver filigree, provides the craft reference. The generated ornament is an original interpretation, not a documented maker's piece.

Reference: [Odisha Tourism · Art and architecture](https://odishatourism.gov.in/content/tourism/en/art-architecture.html)

## Issue 3 · January 2023 — The sea, held close

**Coast / listening**

The shell lies where the water has left it, small against the long patience of the shore. Someone may lift it and bring it close to an ear. Whether the sound is memory or sea hardly matters in that moment. Listening has already made a distance feel smaller.

Cultural connection: A shore-found shell as a poetic object; this image makes no claim about a species, collection practice or ritual conch.

## Issue 2 · December 2022 — Enough to share

**Food / keeping**

The baskets hold grain, but also the thought of meals still to come. Their woven edges turn storage into a visible act of care. Beyond hunger's immediate hour lies another kind of tenderness: keeping something aside, trusting there will be people with whom to open it.

Cultural connection: Grain baskets connect woven utility, food and household memory; no specific basket tradition or community is asserted.

## Issue 1 · November 2022 — Before the first line

**Literature / beginning**

The cup has left a small warmth beside the open notebook. Outside, the day is already underway; on the page, nothing has yet been decided. A beginning need not arrive as a declaration. It can be this modest willingness to sit down, listen, and leave a little room for words.

Cultural connection: An imagined writer's everyday table, opening the collection through attention rather than a biographical claim about an actual poet.
