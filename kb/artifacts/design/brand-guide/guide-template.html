Positioning

A monthly home for poetry.

Rooted in Odisha, welcoming voices in Odia, Hindi and English. Thoughtful, intimate, assured and generous.

The existing domain, publication name and poem links continue. Kabita Live leads the masthead in English. Put language filters beside poem lists on Current issue, Poems and Search, outside the masthead. English navigation is a shared utility, not the publication’s main content language.

Keep

  • The exact Kabita Live spelling.
  • The symbol’s original proportions.
  • A quiet, solid field behind the full logo.

  • Adding letter spacing to Odia or Hindi text.

05
Typography

Three scripts.
Equal care.

Odia / Noto Serif Oriya
ମାଟିର ମହକ
ହୃଦୟର ସ୍ବର

Every Odia heading, poem, name, signature and interface label. Regular 400 for reading; Medium 500 for emphasis. Keep natural character spacing. The family’s technical name contains “Oriya”; readers see “Odia” or “ଓଡ଼ିଆ”.

Hindi / Tiro Devanagari Hindi
कविता
शब्द और स्वर

Titles, poems and editorial prose. Regular 400 upright, with natural letter spacing and 1.8–2 line height. Preserve conjuncts, matras, author spelling and original line breaks.

English / Cormorant + Source Serif
Poetry is an echo,
asking a shadow to dance.

Cormorant Garamond 500 for English titles and mastheads; true italic for large quotations. Source Serif 4 for poems and sustained reading.

Art direction

Wetland reeds, a lotus bud, birds and a small boat establish a soft Odisha connection. Leave an unpainted centre. Use one coherent atmosphere rather than a collection of decorative objects.

This is generated, interpretive artwork. Do not label it authentic Jhoti, Pattachitra or a documentary view. Future commissioned art should carry the artist’s credit.

Movement and restraint

Default to still imagery. The current design uses quiet watercolor edges without a motion control.

Respect reduced-motion preferences. Keep imagery and animation away from the reading column; no parallax, automatic carousels or moving elements behind verse.

The fragrance of earth

A place close enough to feel.

Prefer an Odisha connection when it serves the issue: rain-dark village earth, a real courtyard, Chilika’s reeds and boats, a weaver’s cloth, a coastal evening or an everyday town window. Keep the subject intimate, the light natural and the image realistic.

The voice of the heart

Simple. Poetic. Human.

Give each page one clear purpose and one primary action. Use the same English masthead and navigation on every journal page, with the small leaf-and-water separator beneath it. Keep the footer in one row: author ownership note on the left; Our story, Send a poem and Contact on the right. Let the row wrap on narrow screens. Do not repeat the signature. Use cotton paper at 16% on general pages; assign each poem a stable, varied monsoon or earth wash at 18%, softened on phones. Keep night reading untextured. Group secondary journal links on Our story and design-review links on the review index. Carry the feeling through warm paper, soft earth accents, restrained dividers, unhurried spacing and a quiet watercolor edge. Give inner-page banners 60% text on the left and 40% artwork on the right. Use a distinct soft illustration for each section; repeat it within related pages. Stack on narrow phones. Keep the shared watercolor in the footer. Poem openings may have one distinct illustration alongside the title; preserve a clear, still reading surface for every language.

Image credit rule: record verified place and photographer, commissioned artist and inspiration, or generated-art provenance in Our Story → Credits & colophon. Keep meaningful titles beside images, with local credits only where an asset requires them. Do not relabel an existing generic study as a real Odisha location. Commission regional artists for culturally specific work rather than inventing a traditional pattern. Future location reference: Odisha Tourism — Chilika.

Every cover has a story.

Paper surface: warm ivory with very fine grain and faint fibres. Keep texture beneath the content, retain solid colour swatches and disable texture in night reading, forced-colour mode and print. Pipili Chandua is held in the art library for a future edition.

Choose one cultural anchor and one human feeling. Give each cover an original short narrative, with a separate factual note and source for named places, crafts or people. Let readers open “Story behind the cover”; keep the closed gallery calm.

Rotate landscapes, craft, everyday life, ecology and literary voices. Compare the last six covers for repeated subjects, framing, mood and dominant colour. A sari, a loom and an appliqué panel must tell different stories. Kendu Bana means woodland generally. Portrait tributes need a checked likeness and clear interpretive credit.

Keep the ivory, laterite, sea and hearth palette consistent while allowing each image its own balance. These new treatments do not redefine the original editions’ contents.

Editor’s note: character, then comfort.

Typography should invite the reader into a poem, then give the words room to breathe. Cormorant Garamond Medium gives English mastheads and titles their sculpted, literary character. Composition

  • Keep essential text inside a 6% inset.
  • Wrap long titles; never crop the poet’s name.
  • Use an excerpt only with editorial approval.
  • Recompose stories at 1080 × 1920; do not stretch a square card.

Behaviour

  • Keep the original canonical poem URL.
  • Offer native sharing, copy link and labelled fallbacks.
  • Show success only when copying succeeds.
  • Never claim a social post was sent when a share window merely opened.

Production link previews require server-rendered metadata and public image URLs. The page-design prototype demonstrates the share dialog; external posting remains the reader’s action.

09
Editorial voice

Warm, clear and unhurried.

Speak as a literary host. Be precise about poems, authors, issues and actions.

Invitation

Avoid: “Discover amazing content now!”

“Open this issue.”

Sharing

Avoid: “Make this poem viral!”

“Share this poem.”

Empty search

Avoid: “Oops! Nothing here.”

“No poems match. Try another title or poet.”

Contribution

Avoid: “Submit your masterpiece!”

“Send us your words.”

Preserve author spellings and titles exactly. Language filters select writing; they do not imply translations exist. Have Odia and Hindi interface copy reviewed by fluent editors.
