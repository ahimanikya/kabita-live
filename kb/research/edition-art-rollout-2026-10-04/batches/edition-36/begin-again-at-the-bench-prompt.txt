Poem Almost a Kind of Responsibility by Youngbo Ma; source read fully, use as context only, never render the words:
No one forces you to do this, this craft
is one that must be learned from scratch each time.
Past experience cannot guarantee the future;
it cannot be foolproof. Every time
your rough hands must grope
through a pile of tools, some sharp, some dull.
Every line of each word, you must scrutinize,
pick it up, angle it, like a carpenter
with a red and blue pencil tucked behind your ear.
Wood shavings of words scatter at your feet,
and you occasionally blow away the dust, stop your hands,
listen to the instructions given by the dark mouth.
Perhaps after a bout of hard work,
things begin to take shape.
This is a craft that is not really a craft.
It is gradually being lost; technique can only guarantee
that even a master’s mistake is still remarkable.
Decades of toil might be written off in a single day,
but no one forces you to do this.
The components of the words are your surplus body,
surrounding you, like the buildings
that first grew around the house,
the barns and vestibules, these appendages.
They do not lead you toward death’s footsteps.
Slowing down a little will not bring
the fires of the universe to a halt,
nor will it ease human suffering by a single bit.
No one even notices your work.
But still, you must begin again and again, writing
these words, with caution, hesitation, almost solemnly,
as if the entire universe rests upon your shoulders.
And so, here you sit again, painfully writing
an opening that seems meaningless:

The sun is shining brightly today, things exist.
