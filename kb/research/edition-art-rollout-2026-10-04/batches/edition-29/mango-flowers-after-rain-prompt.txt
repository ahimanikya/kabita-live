Poem MANGO FLOWERS OF MARCH by Bharadwaj Mishra; source read fully, use as context only, never render the words:
Phalguni`s slingshot broke,
the charcoal block she aimed
fell at her feet, and split
and made their own places
to rest on bed of mango-flowers
that spread last night.

Last night`s drizzle was slight,
it brought down all ripe things,
mango leaves,
Spanish-cherry leaves,
it came in her dreams
like Aladdin`s carpet
from “Arabian Nights.”

Arabian nights, no more though
comfort or calm, mostly play blood
these decades for Holi
and Phalguni`s rangoli;
wait, this yellow leaf be her drape
hiding her longing,
the separation, the pining,
her tears of grieving.

Tears of grieving
are the mango-flowers of March,
the aborted births,
the gone-fruits of truth
worshipped as dead,
in vermilion red;
the split charcoal block,
pearled tears on her chest,
this Indian season`s mid-March gift.
