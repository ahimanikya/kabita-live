Poem A Child Again by Eileen Moynihan; source read fully, use as context only, never render the words:
Rambling through the Botanic Gardens
On a mellow Wellington day,
Hand-in-hand with 4 year old,
Inquisitive, granddaughter…
I see the world through her eyes.

We hear the cicadas buzzing hum.
She crouches down to a leaf,
And lifts a cicada onto her hand.
She proudly shows me each part,
While I wonder at her awe.

Skipping along paths happily,
Pockets full of treasures found,
We stop at the duck pond
And quack back at the ducks,
Before we sit and lick ice-creams.

“We’re having a day off,” she says
“Look at the stampede of birds,”
She points at birds chasing crumbs.
Relishing her discovery of words,
I want the joy of being a child again.
