Poem Warmth by Aneek Chatterjee; source read fully, use as context only, never render the words:
In between the green pond
and a hanging sixty watt bulb,
stayed the ghosts and
the haggard garden.

Winters were long and unkind,
without much warm clothes.
Under the drowsy bulb, the clay oven
in the kitchen
turned red every evening when my
sister sat on a tool to prepare bread
and curry for her siblings.
The only window, with generous gaps in the
wooden frame, allowed shivering,
chilly winds.
And children inched closer for warmth,
from the oven and the elder sister.
They also tried to measure: which
one was more round in shape ---
the red oven
or the hand made bread.
Ghosts did not dare to come
near the burning, clay oven.

Winters are still long and shivering.
We have enough warm clothes.
But the red clay oven, hot bread and
sister are lost in the unkempt garden.
Now ghosts play with
all warmth, as we look through
the window gaps, to the
mysterious green pond.
